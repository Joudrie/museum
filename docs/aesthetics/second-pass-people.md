# People: job classes, new parts, props, poses (second pass, main 38cb3e3)

> Part of the aesthetics audit (29 Oct 2026, second pass, main 38cb3e3). Overview: [../AESTHETICS-AUDIT.md](../AESTHETICS-AUDIT.md). Visual version with every screenshot: https://claude.ai/artifact/5TdJk17Mx4g36tciuXPfvW (private to the owner).


Scope: the people content from PRs #190, #192 and #194 (`src/people/parts.js`, `src/people/classes.js`, `src/people/index.js`, the `art.js` hooks, the art.html People tab, the builder's People lists), plus the people side of #195 (named friendship NPCs in `src/progression/data/npcs.json`, `progression/ui/islandlife.js`, and the cosmetic hats and outfits in `progression/ui/lookfx.js`). That is 137 classes and 312 variants, 29 new hats, 16 new hairstyles, 7 facial hair options, 6 glasses, 24 outfits, 14 accessories, 4 builds, 5 leg and 2 feet options, 84 carried props, 33 ground props, 2 shoulder pets and 63 work poses.

Method: every variant was rolled 4 times through the real `personFor` / `personRows` / `poseRows` code in Node. All frames (down, side, up, both steps, both pose frames) were rendered to contact sheets at 3x (`shots/people2/classes-00..31-3x.png`, one row per variant: the pose or walk frames, then 3 more rolls). Every new part was rendered on a plain body at 4-12x. Then one person per class was placed on a test island and screenshotted in the running game at 390x844, dpr 3 (`crowd-phone-00..05.png`). The player cosmetics, a named NPC talk, the art.html People tab and the builder list were checked live too.
Shots are in `docs/aesthetics/shots/people/`; file names below link to them.

The 312 people all sit on the old 20x27 body, so P-01..P-20 (blank face, box torso, side arm, black shoe plank and so on) apply to every one of them. They are not repeated below, except where a new part makes one of them worse.

## A. Where the new people are, and where they are not

- **[P-124] None of the 137 classes appears anywhere in the shipped game** (High). `src/people/index.js` `addPeople` makes them builder tiles (`t:npc_<class>_<variant>`), and `IslandScene` draws them only when a map holds those tiles. No shipped map does: grep finds no `npc_` in `src/maps/*.json`. The only hit is the builder's what's-new list. The museum's own janitor, guard, guide, barista and carpenter still come from the old `visitorLook` (RoomScene.js ~139). So does every Main Island, Island One and expedition islander, and every named friendship NPC. So the job sprites the user will see are the old, broken ones, and the new janitor (coveralls, cap and a mop pose) exists only in the builder. → Make the museum staff and the islanders on the shipped islands use the classes: janitor.mop in the rooms, fisherman.dock on the Main Island dock, archaeologist on the dig islands, piratecrew on exp-pirate. Retire `visitorLook` for staff. Shot: [crowd-phone-00.png](shots/people2/crowd-phone-00.png) (the test island built for this audit).
- **[P-125] Named friendship NPCs wear a random visitor look that contradicts their job** (High). `islandlife.js` `placeNamed` spawns each NPC through `islander(..., { kind: npc.kind })`, so they get a museum-visitor recolour. Wen the "Pond keeper" is an elder in a lilac gown with no net or bucket. Mara the "Harbour sailor" is a `casual` in a sweater. Ines the "Diver" is a `nerd` and always gets the skull glasses (P-23). Tal the "Castaway" is a `tourist` with a camera. Yuki the "Temple keeper" is a `superfan`, Rook the "Lookout" is a `grump`. Matching classes already exist: `sailor`, `diver`, `castaway`, `templekeeper`, `piratecrew.lookout`, `fountaincleaner`. The NPCs you are meant to befriend over months are the least readable people on the island. → Give every NPC in npcs.json a `person: 'diver.tank'`-style class id plus fixed colours, so each is a unique, styled member of their job class. Also give them their own small mark, such as a scarf colour or a hat feather. Shot: [named-wen-talk.png](shots/people2/named-wen-talk.png), [named-mara-world.png](shots/people2/named-mara-world.png).
- **[P-126] All 9 cosmetic hats are the same two flat rectangles in a different colour** (High). `lookfx.js` `PlayerLook.update` draws every hat with `g.fillRect`: a 4 px brim bar plus a crown 60% as wide, with an outline in near-black #1d1714. Straw hat, Fisher beanie, Bucket hat, Captain's cap, Flower crown, Lantern hat, Winter antlers, Witch hat and Curator's crown all come out as the same purple, yellow or blue pillbox top hat. The witch hat is not pointed, the antlers are a brown box, and the crown is a yellow box with a 2x2 red dot. The width `displayWidth * 0.55` (20.24 px) is off the pixel grid, so the hat's edges don't line up with the sprite's pixels. These are shop and rank rewards the player pays for, and they look like debug boxes. Real pixel versions of most of them now exist in `MORE_HATS` (bucket, captain, pompom, fur, kingcrown). → Bake the cosmetic into the player's look (`look.hat = 'bucket'` with `T` set to the cosmetic colour) through the normal `makeCharacter` path. Draw the witch hat, antlers and lantern hat as real HAT_ROWS entries. Shot: [player-cosmetics-sheet.png](shots/people2/player-cosmetics-sheet.png), [cosm-08.png](shots/people2/cosm-08.png).
- **[P-127] Cosmetic outfits tint the whole player, so faces turn yellow, grey or pink** (High). `PlayerLook` calls `pl.setTint(mix(0xffffff, outfit, 0.45))` on the whole sprite. The Yellow oilskin gives a yellow face and yellow hair. The Gallery tuxedo turns the skin grey-brown, the Spring kimono and Regatta blazer turn the face pink or red, and the Frostline parka turns it ashen. The Ghost sheet barely changes anything, so it looks unequipped. → Swap the outfit's `c` palette letter (or `look.outfit = 'oilskins'` / `'suit'` / `'parka'`) and re-bake the player. Never tint skin. The ghost sheet needs its own sheet sprite (see P-84). Shot: [player-cosmetics-sheet.png](shots/people2/player-cosmetics-sheet.png) (bottom row).
- **[P-128] Cosmetics disappear when you walk indoors** (Med). Only `IslandScene` creates `PlayerLook`, so the hat you bought vanishes in the museum and the iso room. → Same fix as P-126: make the cosmetic part of the baked look, so every scene shows it.

## C. Carried props (`CARRY_DRAW`, parts.js:1080)

- **[P-137] In side view every pole, handle and tool is drawn through the face** (High). `pole()` (parts.js:1037) and the custom carriers put the stick at `x = hx + 2` (column 17, the middle of the side head), from `hy - 10` (eye level) down. Or carry them over the far shoulder, drawn before the body. Shot: [carry-side-through-face-6x.png](shots/people2/carry-side-through-face-6x.png).
- **[P-138] Long props flip between upright and level as you turn** (Med). - **[P-139] A worker's tool vanishes when they turn to talk to you** (Med). `IslandScene.moveIslanders` swaps a posed person to their plain `_side` / `_down` texture while you chat. On the all-white painter the roller is a white bone in the air. → Hold them low at the side, head down, or raised with a drawn arm (as in the `dust` pose, which works).
- **[P-141] The trash bag is a blue ball, the radio a black square, the tank a white fridge** (Low). `trashbag` uses `B` (#3d6fb6), so with default colours it is a round blue ball. - **[P-142] The dog prop is a brown teddy and doesn't match the game's real dogs** (Med). `GROUND_PICS.dog` (1417) is a 6x7 tan square with two black dots and ears, used for the guard dog, the dog walker and the sled dog ("husky"). ## Systemic issues (new content)

- **More parts on the same broken body.** Every class inherits the blank face, stub legs, side-belly hand and one-frame side walk, and the new parts make some of these worse (boots, bare feet, broad build, side-view poles).
- **Props are drawn where the code has a free cell, not where hands are.** Belly patches, a pole column through the side face, ground props bottom-aligned at the ankle, and the tool removed when talking. There is no per-view hand anchor or layer order (in front of the body or behind it).
- **Scale ignores the world.** Carts, bikes, tents, bins and prams are all 8 px tall. Props are sized to fit a 32 px cell, not to sit next to a person.
- **Texture by modulo.** Hairnet, cornrows, slick, locs, wavy, braid and the mummy wrap use `% 2` / `% 3` stripe or checker patterns, the same waffle look as the old buzz cut and curly hair.
- **Colour defaults leak.** The brown `U` makes hat bands, pom-poms, fur flaps and veils brown, the default `g` mustard shows on wetsuit stripes and sailor collars, the default `X` red on gloves, and pure-white `Q` on tiaras, lanyards, pirate hats and the horseshoe shine.
- **Near-black for every dark detail.** Mask rims, goggle straps, eye patch, radio, tablet, TV camera and welding visor are all `k`, so they read as holes or blindfolds, like the old shades.
- **Duplicates over classes.** Several job classes share one outfit and hat (seven hard hat plus hi-vis classes), against the "silhouette first" rule.
- **Two parallel people systems.** Museum `visitorLook`, the builder classes, the named NPCs and the player cosmetics are separate code paths with separate looks, so fixes in one (a proper janitor) don't reach the others.

## Keep

- The Old Salt fisherman (yellow oilskins plus sou'wester), the Breton-striped dock angler and boatman, the flannel plaid and the varsity jacket read at a glance.
- The road flagger's stop/slow sign, the tour guide's flag and umbrella, the toddler and kid balloon and kite, the donor's big cheque, the surveyor's tripod and the lifebuoy all read at phone size.
- The police cap, flat cap and chef's toque, the hood, the overalls' crossed back straps, the backpack's back view, the mailbag, the life jacket and the captain's double-breasted coat are good bases.
- The square glasses (frame plus bridge plus side arm) and the big and wizard beards are the best new face parts.
- The data-driven class system (variants, seeded rolls, a per-class palette) and the two-frame pose framework are worth keeping under a redrawn body.
